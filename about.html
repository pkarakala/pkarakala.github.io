About

Quantum Computing, Photonics, HPC, and ML — building systems that scale under real hardware constraints

Pranav Reddy

Electrical & Computer Engineering · UC Santa Barbara

I build systems where High Performance Computing actually matters — from ML-based quantum error correction decoders that outperform Bayesian methods at 96% accuracy, to parallel simulation infrastructure distributed across 40+ CPU cores, to real-time RAG pipelines powering enterprise supply chain operations.

My work spans quantum control, photonics, and machine learning — grounded in computer architecture, hardware-aware algorithm design, and the kind of engineering where latency, scalability, and reliability aren't optional.

🔬 How I Create Impact

Advancing Quantum Computing

Built ML-based decoders that outperform traditional Bayesian methods under realistic noise — achieving 96% accuracy on continuous quantum error correction. This work pushes toward fault-tolerant quantum systems that can operate reliably at scale.

Engineering Scalable Systems

I design simulation and data pipelines that handle thousands of trajectories with proper train/test isolation, windowed time-series processing, and reproducible benchmarking. Systems built to scale from prototype to production.

Bridging Theory and Practice

From Wonham filters to GRU networks, I benchmark principled baselines against data-driven approaches — identifying exactly where and why learned models outperform analytical ones. Research that translates to engineering decisions.

Experimental Rigor

Demonstrated Bell inequality violations (S=2.35), single-photon antibunching (g²(0)=0.094), and photonic quantum gate modeling. I bring lab-grade precision to both experimental and computational work.

Collaborative Research

Co-authored research with peers, contributed to group lab experiments, and built open-source tools with 124+ unit tests. I write code that other engineers can trust, extend, and build on.

Next-Gen Architecture

Passionate about pushing system performance forward — whether that's hardware-aware algorithm design, real-time decoding under latency constraints, or building the infrastructure that makes Quantum Computing practical.
