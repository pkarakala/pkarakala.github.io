About

Quantum Computing, Photonics, ML Accelerator Systems, and Fiber Optics — building systems that scale under real hardware constraints

Pranav Reddy

Electrical & Computer Engineering · UC Santa Barbara

I build systems where performance actually matters — from ML-based quantum error correction decoders that outperform Bayesian methods at 96% accuracy, to a full ML accelerator software stack covering graph compilation, kernel optimization, and cycle-accurate hardware simulation, to parallel simulation infrastructure distributed across 40+ CPU cores.

My work spans quantum control, photonics, ML systems, and computer architecture — from torch.fx graph compilers and CUDA kernel codegen to anti-resonant hollow-core fiber analysis and real-time RAG pipelines in production. I'm drawn to the kind of engineering where latency, scalability, and hardware constraints shape every design decision.

🔬 How I Create Impact

Advancing Quantum Computing

Built a 4-phase ML decoder pipeline achieving 96% accuracy on continuous Quantum Error Correction. Adaptive online learning maintains 82% accuracy under hardware drift — a path toward self-calibrating fault-tolerant quantum systems. 244 unit tests across all phases.

Engineering Scalable Systems

Built a full ML accelerator software stack from scratch — graph compilation via torch.fx, Triton-style kernel optimization with loop tiling, and cycle-accurate hardware simulation that identifies compute vs memory bottlenecks. Also designed simulation pipelines handling thousands of quantum trajectories with proper train/test isolation and reproducible benchmarking.

Bridging Theory and Practice

From Wonham filters to GRU networks, from brute-force fiber simulation to analytic models that predict confinement loss from geometry alone — I benchmark principled baselines against data-driven approaches, identifying exactly where and why one outperforms the other. Research that translates to engineering decisions.

Experimental Rigor

Demonstrated Bell inequality violations (S=2.35), single-photon antibunching (g²(0)=0.094), and photonic quantum gate modeling. Validated AR-HCF performance against traditional SMF using VPI Photonics simulation across power, distance, and frequency sweeps. Lab-grade precision in both experimental and computational work.

Collaborative Research

Co-authored the CQEC ML decoder project, led methods and analysis for a 4-person fiber optics research team (ECE 135), and contributed to group quantum optics experiments. I write code and reports that other engineers can trust, extend, and build on.

Next-Gen Architecture

From ML compiler IRs and CUDA kernel codegen to anti-resonant hollow-core fiber modeling with VPI Photonics — I work across the stack where hardware constraints drive software design. Whether it's memory planning for on-chip SRAM or predicting confinement loss scaling at 1/R⁴, I build tools that connect architecture decisions to measurable performance.
